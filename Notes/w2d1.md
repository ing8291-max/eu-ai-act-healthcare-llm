## What I learned

**1. Two conditions, joined by "both".**
6(1): high-risk "where **both** of the following conditions are
fulfilled":
- (a) the system is a safety component of a product, or is itself a
  product, covered by Union harmonisation legislation in **Annex I**
- (b) that product is **required to undergo third-party conformity
  assessment** pursuant to the same Annex I legislation

Logical AND — both must hold. If either fails, this route does not
apply. That does not settle the question: 6(2) is a separate route,
still unread.

**2. (b) is not a consequence of (a).**
I first read this as: match Annex I, therefore undergo assessment.
Wrong. They are independent conditions. The assessment requirement
originates in the Annex I legislation ("pursuant to"), not in the
AI Act — 6(1)(b) only checks whether that requirement already exists.

So (a) can hold while (b) fails: a product may fall under Annex I
legislation yet not require third-party assessment. If (b) followed
automatically from (a), splitting them would be pointless.

**3. Conditions (a) and (b) are lookups, not risk assessments.**
Neither asks how dangerous the system is. (a) checks membership under
Annex I legislation; (b) checks whether an assessment requirement
exists. First live instance of the article-to-annex dependency I
mapped on W1D1 — 6(1) cannot resolve anything on its own.

**4. Where `risk` from Art. 3(2) actually enters.**
(a) and (b) never use it. 1b brings in a qualitative version —
"failure or malfunctioning would endanger health and safety" — but not
the probability-and-severity structure from Art. 3(2). Where that
definition gets operationalised is still unknown. Watch for it in
Art. 9.

**5. 1a / 1b / 1c are Digital Omnibus insertions — all marked `new`.**
First encounter with the inserted-paragraph pattern noted on W1D1.
Anything written before 27 July 2026 will not contain these.

**6. 1a and 1b form a rule and its counter-rule.**
- **1a** — systems used *solely* for non-safety purposes (user
  assistance, performance optimisation, service efficiency, automation,
  convenience, quality control) do **not** qualify as safety components.
- **1b** — "Notwithstanding paragraph 1a", systems whose **failure or
  malfunctioning would endanger health and safety** **do** qualify.

1b overrides 1a. The operative question is not what the system is
*used for* but what happens when it *fails*. A convenience feature
whose failure endangers health is still a safety component.

**7. 1c is an exclusion keyed to the reason for assessment.**
A product required to undergo third-party assessment *solely* for
risks other than health and safety — the text names radio spectrum
distribution and electromagnetic interference not affecting health and
safety — does **not** satisfy condition (b).

So (b) is not "is an assessment required?" but "is it required
*because of* health and safety?".

Four gates in series. Any single failure exits the route. Exit means
"not high-risk *by this path*" — 6(2) is evaluated separately.

**9. Exclusions are half the logic.**
1a and 1c are carve-outs; 1b is a carve-back-in. A classifier built
only from inclusion criteria would return false positives. Phase 2
design note.

**10. 6(1) applies from 2 August 2028.**
Per Art. 113(c)(ii), shown inline on the article page. Same pattern as
Art. 22 on W1D1: application dates differ article by article.

**11. W1D2 hypothesis, now partly confirmed.**
On 09-01 I wrote that Section 1's title "suggests" high-risk is
determined by a procedure rather than defined. 6(1) confirms it for
this route. Whether 6(2) works the same way is still unknown.

## What confused me
I first read 6(1) as "if it threatens safety, it's high-risk" — a
judgment call. It isn't. (a) is a lookup against Annex I legislation;
(b) asks whether third-party assessment is required; 1c narrows that
to assessments required *because of* health and safety. None of these
is my own risk judgment. The only judgment-shaped test is 1b, and it
asks about failure consequences, not intended use.

Recurring pattern in my misreadings: I keep reading sequential
paragraphs as cause and effect when they are parallel conditions.
Watch for this.

`solely` carries weight in both 1a and 1c. Both carve-outs collapse
the moment a system has any health-and-safety dimension.

Open: the gate order in the diagram above is my own arrangement, not
the Act's. The text lists (a), (b), then 1a–1c as qualifiers. To
re-check once Annex I is read.
