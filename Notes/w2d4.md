# W2D4 — 2026-09-10

## Read
Art. 6(2), and the structure of 6(3) and 6(4).

## What I learned

**1. 6(2) has no conditions.**
One sentence: systems referred to in Annex III are high-risk.
No third-party assessment, no Annex I. Where 6(1) was four gates in
series, 6(2) is bare list membership.

**2. 6(3) is an exit, but not the one I first assumed.**
I read the first subparagraph as "if it isn't dangerous, it's excluded".
That is not how it works. The second subparagraph states the exemption
applies "where **any** of the following conditions is fulfilled" — four
named types (a)-(d), and one suffices.

So the exemption is a type test, not a self-assessment of danger.
I cannot argue my way out by asserting low risk; the system has to fit
one of the listed shapes.

Same structural move as 6(1), which anchored entry to the Annex I list
rather than to a judgment of harm.

**3. Profiling overrides the exit.**
A system performing profiling of natural persons is always high-risk
regardless of 6(3). Same override pattern as 1a/1b in the 6(1) route.

**4. 6(4) creates a deliverable either way.**
A provider concluding a system is *not* high-risk must document that
assessment before placing it on the market, and is subject to the
registration obligation in Art. 49(2). Documentation must be produced
to authorities on request.

Concluding "not high-risk" is not a null action. Either outcome
produces work.
