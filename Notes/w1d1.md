## What I read
Table of contents of the EU AI Act (consolidated text, 27 July 2026),

## What I learned
**1. Chapter III is where my project lives.**
Arts. 6–49, about 40% of the Act. It breaks into five sections:

| Section | Articles | Topic |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | 6–7 | Classification as high-risk |
| S2 | 8–15 | Requirements |
| S3 | 16–27 | Obligations of providers and deployers |
| S4 | 28–39 | Notifying authorities and notified bodies |
| S5 | 40–49 | Standards, conformity assessment, registration |

**2. Article count does not equal importance.**
S4 has 12 articles, S2 has 8. But S2 contains the entire technical
specification — risk management, data governance, technical
documentation, logging, accuracy. S2 is a week of reading; S4 is an hour.

**3. Annexes and articles are not one-to-one.**
Annex VIII is defined at Art. 49 (S5) but also invoked by Art. 22 (S3).
References form a graph, not a tree.

**4. Application dates differ within the same chapter.**
Art. 22 applies from 2 December 2027 (Annex III systems) and
2 August 2028 (Annex I systems), per Art. 113(c). "The AI Act applies
from August 2026" is too coarse to be useful.

## What confused me

I tried to map every annex-to-article reference by hand and it doesn't
scale. Reading annex titles alone ("referred to in Article __") only
captures backward references and misses forward ones from article text.
This is a parsing problem, not a reading problem.

Logged as a Phase 2 tooling task: `annex-reference-mapper`.

I also don't yet know why some annexes cluster in S1, S2 and S5 while
S3 and S4 have almost none. Current guess: annexes hold lists, forms
and procedures, so they appear where the Act defines *what* rather than
*who*. Unverified.
