## Read
Art. 10 — Data and data governance (consolidated text, 2026-07-27).

## What I learned

**1. Art. 10(6) narrows the scope for my system.**
I do not train models — I call an LLM API. Under 10(6), paragraphs 2, 3
and 4 apply only to the *testing* data sets in that case. So my RAG
corpus is not the regulated artifact; my Golden Dataset is.

Same documents, different roles:
- used for retrieval  -> not training data -> out of scope
- used for scoring    -> testing data      -> in scope

**2. 10(2) and 10(3) are different kinds of obligation.**
10(2) lists eight governance *practices* (a)–(h) — things I must do.
10(3) lists *properties* the data must have. I had only read 10(3) and
nearly missed the eight.

**3. 10(3) has two tiers of strictness, and my weakness sits in the
strict tier.**
"relevant, sufficiently representative" carry no qualifier.
"free of errors and complete" are prefixed with "to the best extent
possible".

My synthetic corpus is weakest on representativeness — the one criterion
with no allowance. Errors and gaps can be defended as best-effort;
representativeness cannot. This makes point 4 mandatory rather than
optional.

**4. 10(2)(h) requires stating known gaps and how they will be addressed.**
My corpus is LLM-generated, not real clinical text. Recording that
limitation is compliance, not weakness — and the planned MIMIC-IV-Note
validation is the stated remedy.
